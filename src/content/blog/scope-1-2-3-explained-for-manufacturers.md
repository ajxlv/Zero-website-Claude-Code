The Greenhouse Gas Protocol sorts a company’s emissions into three scopes. The split exists so that every tonne is counted once, by the right company, and so you can see which emissions you control directly and which you only influence.

## Scope 1: what you burn

Direct emissions from sources you own or control: fuel in boilers and furnaces, diesel in generators, LPG in canteens and process heat, petrol and diesel in company vehicles, and refrigerant leaks. The data usually lives in fuel invoices, purchase records and maintenance logs.

## Scope 2: what you buy to power the plant

Indirect emissions from purchased electricity, steam, heat or cooling. They happen at the power station, but your consumption causes them. Monthly utility bills are the main source, multiplied by a grid emission factor.

## Scope 3: everything else in your value chain

All other indirect emissions, upstream and downstream: purchased raw materials, packaging, inbound and outbound freight, business travel, employee commuting, waste, and the use and disposal of the products you sell. The GHG Protocol groups them into 15 categories.

> For most manufacturers, Scope 3 is the largest share, and the one you can see least.

Scope 3 is hard because the data sits with suppliers. Most companies start with spend-based estimates, then replace the biggest categories with supplier-specific data over time.

## Where to start

1. Build a Scope 1 and 2 baseline from a full year of fuel and utility records.
2. Screen all 15 Scope 3 categories and find the three that matter most.
3. Ask your top suppliers by spend for primary data.
4. Set targets, then report to the framework your customers or regulator ask for, such as BRSR, GRI or CDP.
